Interpretation

The code performs hierarchical clustering on the standardized wine data (Wine_data) using different linkage methods:

  • agnes_single: Implements single linkage (nearest point approach) that identifies the distance as the shortest distance between two clusters.

  • agnes_complete: In Lacking a definitive rule, K-song uses the ‘complete linkage’ (also known as ‘farthest point approach’), according to which the distance between clusters is equal to the largest distance between two points belonging to these clusters.

  • agnes_ward: Uses Ward’s method that selects a clustering that minimizes the total sum of squares within clusters, putting together clusters that produce the lowest increase in variance.

  • agnes_average: Uses average linkage where the measure of dissimilarity between a cluster of points is the average of the dissimilarity between all the points belonging to different clusters.

To compare the Agglomerative Coefficient (AC) from AGNES with the Divisive Coefficient (DC) from DIANA, let’s first understand what these coefficients represent:

Agglomerative Coefficient (AC)

Agglomerative Coefficient quantifies the level of compactness within clusters when the hierarchical Agglomerative Nested Clusterwise Equalization (AGNES) is being used. The greater the number it means that the clusters are stricter that is the objects inside the cluster are closer to each other more similar to other objects in the same cluster.

Divisive Coefficient (DC)

It is an internal measure that quantifies the level of togetherness of objects within a cluster in the course of divisive hierarchical clustering (DIANA). But, for DC, greater is the value of DC, more compact are the clusters that result, and therefore, it is more efficient in terms of partitioning the data into sensible clusters.

………………………Determine Appropriate ClusterinTechnique…………………….

AGNES :

AGNES is a bottom up hierarchical clustering method. It begins with each data point being a different cluster, and then it merges the clusters sequentially according to any chosen linkage measure (Ward, single, complete linkage etc.) with the process ending when all data points are formed into a single cluster. The goal is to create cohesive groups, with each linkage method providing a different way of measuring cohesion:

  • Single linkage: Connects those clusters which has the minimum distance between two points in that cluster.

  • Complete linkage: This is achieved by mergin clusters based on the bignness of the maximum distance.

  • Ward’s method: Concerned with tight clustering and less variance within clusters or small spread of the clusters.

AGNES gives an Agglomerative Coefficient denoting the degree of compactness of each cluster; a higher value of AC means that points within a cluster are closely related.

DIANA :

DIANA is a global hierarchical clustering algorithm and the one that works on a top-down manner. It starts with the condition where the whole data set is condensed into one cluster, then it divides the clusters till the most numerous point is split first. This method gives very dispersed clusters at the initial stages and sometimes gives subgroups possessing peculiarity. DIANA generates a Divisive Coefficient (DC) to measure how much the clusters are split, consequently, the higher DC shows better cluster separation.

  • DIANA: It is noteworthy that DIANA’s splitting strategy itself results in a smaller number of highly separated substantives at the initial stages and is more appropriate for cases when it is necessary to work with outliers or to determine correspondingly clearly separated groups of variables. K-Means Clustering

  • K-Means is an instance of a centroid-based as it clusters” data into a predetermined number of clusters commonly represented as K. It means that it labels each point to the cluster with its closest centroid, computes a new centroid based on all the points within each cluster, and it repeats the steps until it reaches a certain level of consistency. The main objective of K-Means is to reduce the within cluster variation.

  • Advantages: K-Means clustering is really effective and suitable for the large datasets. It is particularly good for clusters that are nearly spherical and the sizes of which do not differ significantly. It is good when both clusters are separated and distributed in an equal manner.

  • Limitations: K-Means assumes that clusters are convex and isotropic i.e, the same in all directions. This can be a limitation since in some dataset cases the clusters do not necessarily have the normal geometrical shape of circle, square, or triangle and some areas of some formed clusters may be more densely populated than others. This is so because outliers affect centroids and in most cases the algorithm is very receptive to this kind of observations. Moreover, the value of K must be defined in advance, and the method might only find local optima, which is the performance can be affected by the choice of initial cluster centroids.

  • Suitability for Wine Dataset: However, if the structure of wine data reveals clusters that are crisp, well-separated and nearly spherical, perhaps K-Means may work. However, unlike K-Means, it considers clusters with different density and irregular shapes in the wine dataset (due to different chemical makeup and methods of production) while it does not allow an analyst to miss certain groupings which could be visible to hierarchical clustering methods.

DBSCAN (Clusters Analysis of Spatial Density of Applications for a Set with Noise Density)

  • DBSCAN is one of the most popular density-based clustering algorithms that clusters together points that are dense and tightly grouped by using two parameters: epsilon, which sets the range within which the points are neighbors, and minPts which shows the number of points within epsilon in order to form a cluster. Item that have not been grouped into any cluster are referred to as noise. DBSCAN can find clusters of any number of clusters and can find clusters of arbitrary shapes, and it is also insensitive to noise.

  • Advantages: DBSCAN is losing its effectiveness in case of irregularly shaped clusters and more importantly in case when some of the clusters are sparse than others while K-Means is more rigid in its approach. It also identifies and labels outliers as noise this is important when filtering out unnecessary data from the analysis. Hence, DBSCAN is well suited for a dataset where clusters have low density separation or contain clusters with low density elongated irregular shapes.

  • Limitations: DBSCAN has sensitive parameters: epsilon and minPts. As observed, when the parameters are chosen poorly, DBSCAN can either fail to identify clusters of significance or merge individual clusters. It also performs poorly when the clusters inside the data set partition have dissimilar densities.

  • Suitability for Wine Dataset: DBSCAN is best applied on wine dataset, especially if its dataset has somehow clusters with multi-uneven density shapes. That means to use it to determine different clusters of wines without having to assume that the clusters are spherical in nature is workable. Also, since DBSCAN can find outliers they will be able to identify the wines which may not belong to any cluster at all making the analysis more flexible and insightful compared to the use of K-Means.

Conclusion

For the wine dataset, DBSCAN appears to be the more suitable alternative to hierarchical methods due to its flexibility in handling clusters with different densities and its robustness against outliers. K-Means could be applied if simplicity is prioritized and the dataset shows uniform, spherical clusters. However, given the hierarchical relationships in wine characteristics and potential density variations, DBSCAN would likely offer a more insightful clustering solution by capturing both the core clusters and unique outliers in the dataset.
